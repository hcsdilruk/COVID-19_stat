Regression analysis — all-available-age sensitivity analysis
The extract omitted the age-imputation indicator. The proposal identified 25,376 estimated-age rows before report selection. All estimates below use ages as supplied. Primary recorded-age analyses require the matching archive.

We retained the lowest numeric entry order for each report ID, then selected female or male reports with one named manufacturer. A flag indicated hospitalization or death marked Yes. Neither marked Yes includes unknown clinical status. All three models used 195,953 reports. Female, Pfizer and neither flag marked Yes were reference categories.

After adjustment for sex and manufacturer, flagged reports had a mean age 17.40 years higher (HC3 95% CI 17.16–17.64) and a conditional median age 16 years higher. The 200-resample percentile interval for the median contrast collapsed to 16–16 years. Repeated ages and estimated values limit interpretation of this interval. The logistic model with quadratic age fitted better than linear age alone (likelihood-ratio chi-square 1450.82, df 1, p < .001). Male reports had 1.69 times the adjusted odds of a recorded flag compared with female reports (95% CI 1.64–1.73).

Among adults the adjusted mean contrast was 12.99 years, the median contrast 14 years and the male-to-female odds ratio 1.72. Changing entry order or including mixed manufacturer lists yielded similar contrasts. The small Novavax group had 13 flagged reports among 196. Interpret its coefficient cautiously. Maximum Cook distance was 0.0043. Passive reporting, uncertain clinical status, age imputation and possible dependence across report IDs limit inference. These results describe reporting associations. Population risk and causation cannot be estimated from these reports.
